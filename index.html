About

Engineer, maker, and someone
who keeps a camera nearby.

I am an Electrical Engineering undergraduate at Northeastern University, working at the point where circuits, code, and physical fabrication meet. Most of what I build ends up being all three at once — a board, the firmware that drives it, and an enclosure that has to survive contact with real users.

I spend my working hours in Northeastern’s makerspaces. At FYELIC I support 20–30 students a shift through circuits, Raspberry Pi systems, Python, and CAD problems; at the EXP Makerspace I train users on the Bantam Tools PCB mill, soldering stations, 3D printers, and laser cutters. Teaching other people to debug their projects has made me considerably better at debugging my own.

Outside the lab I shoot photography — mostly low light, wildlife, and whatever happens to be in front of me. It is a useful counterweight to engineering: no error messages, just whether the frame works or it does not.
