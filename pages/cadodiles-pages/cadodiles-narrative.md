# Project Narrative

CADodiles began as an idea to make STEM review more engaging for 5th grade students by pairing a digital trivia game with real, physical rewards. Rather than a screen-only quiz app, the goal was to build something that responded to a correct answer the way an arcade game might: instantly, visually, and physically.

The project moved through three main stages of development: software (building the Kivy-based trivia interface), hardware (wiring the Raspberry Pi to a servo and LED strip for feedback), and mechanical design (modeling and fabricating an enclosure in SolidWorks that could safely and reliably house all of it). Each stage informed the next — for example, the physical constraints of the servo mechanism shaped the enclosure's internal layout, and the enclosure's final form shaped how the touchscreen and wiring were routed.

By the end of the project, CADodiles was a fully working, self-contained trivia game: a student could select a topic, answer a series of questions on the touchscreen, and receive real-time physical feedback for each answer — a small but complete demonstration of how software, electronics, and mechanical design come together in a single engineering product.
